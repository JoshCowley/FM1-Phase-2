FM1 Rack on the FM-1: Firmware Manual

Nov 11, 2026 · Josh Cowley

FM1 Rack turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a small modular synthesizer built from Mutable Instruments' open module code. Each sound is a short chain of modules: a source (Plaits or Braids), a shaper (Rings or empty), a filter that is always there, two modulators, two effects and an amplifier. You play it from the keys, shape it with the knobs, and link anything to anything through a modulation matrix. This manual covers the firmware on the FM-1; the browser version has its own manual, the FM1 Rack Manual.

This is a test version (update 43). The sound engine and the first menus have been played on an FM-1. The newer screens, the FUNC module, the 67 factory presets, the button shortcuts, STYLE, the smoother preset and knob changes, LEARN on SEL + EDIT, the chords' and arpeggio's FM-1 / SEND switches, the finer pictures, the BASS zone and the sequencer's editing (lasso, copy and paste, dynamics, articulations) and the click track have so far run only in a computer simulator of the FM-1. Keep the recovery steps in section 2 to hand.

1. Quick start

  1. Install with the script in section 2. The FM-1 starts on HOME with the first preset, INIT.
  2. Choose a sound: turn PRESET. The new preset loads when you stop turning. ALGORITHM jumps a whole category (keys, bass, lead, pads, textures).
  3. Play the keys. They respond to how hard you play.
  4. Shape it: on HOME, K1–K4 are the preset's four macros. Each starts in the middle, where the preset sounds as designed, and moves something useful (brightness, character, space, motion). The name under each says what it moves.
  5. Look inside: press EDIT for the sound source, ENV for the envelopes, the filter and the shaper (hold ENV and turn K1: the filter sweep), LFO for the modulation, FX for the effects. On each page, K1–K4 set the four parameters shown and SELECT turns the page.
  6. Undo: hold EDIT to put back the last change.
  7. Keep it: press SAVE, then SAVE again. Section 12 covers names and slots.
  8. Back home: press HOME.

2. Installing, and getting back to stock

Before you install

  • Do the recovery round trip once, so you know you can get back to the stock firmware:
    1. Plug the FM-1 straight into the Mac, with no hub.
    2. In Chrome or Edge, open Felucca's web installer (hugelton.github.io/Felucca) and install Felucca.
    3. Play it, then choose Return to official V15.
  • Run the benchmark once (install_bench.sh): its top line must read FPU OK: HARDWARE FLOAT, EXACT. FM1 Rack needs the FM-1's hardware floating point.
  • Quit anything else that uses MIDI: DAWs, and browser tabs with Web MIDI.

Installing

Everything is in the FM1-Rack folder (Documents/iClaude/Projects/FM1-Rack). In Terminal:

sh /Users/josh/Documents/iClaude/Projects/FM1-Rack/install_rack.sh

The script:

  1. checks that test-kit/packages/fm1ph2.fwsc is exactly the file that was built (its SHA-256 fingerprint);
  2. installs the installer's MIDI library if needed;
  3. fetches fm1-base's installer at a fixed version;
  4. finds the FM-1 and installs.

Don't unplug the FM-1 until it says it's done. The firmware identifies itself as FM-1_7800.

By hand, from the repo's firmware/packages folder:

shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS
python3 ~/fm1-base/tools/fm1_install.py fm1ph2.fwsc

Getting back to the stock firmware

In order of what to try:

  1. Felucca's web installer again, then Return to official V15.
  2. fm1-base's installer page: "Back to the stock firmware".
  3. After two failed starts the FM-1 comes up in safe mode (no audio, USB on), which accepts an update.
  4. After four, the chip's own update mode: M-VAVE's M-UPGRADE (Downloads › PC Software) restores the stock firmware.

These steps come from fm1-base and Felucca. None has been needed with FM1 Rack so far.

SELECT still turns the dots either way. A button counts as held only after 0.6 s, so a quick press is never taken for a hold.

4. Reading the screen

Every page has the same frame, top to bottom.

  • The status strip:
    • a light for each voice, lit by its envelope;
    • the cores and voice mode: 2C POLY on two cores, 1C on one, 1C* if the second core didn't start;
    • SHED n in gold when n voices were dropped to save processor time, HEAVY in red when a preset is muted (section 13);
    • CPU, a bar and a number: gold above 70 %, red above 85 %;
    • RAM: how much of the sound memory the preset uses;
    • the octave (O+1 and so on; green while keys are held);
    • the output meter (red near clipping).
  • The header: the page's icon in its section's colour, the page's name, and a dot for each page within it.

The look (update 53, chosen by A/B): true black (update 55: more contrast between the background and the graphics, brighter labels and colours) with greys of a slight warmth; the pictures sit straight on the page, with no boxes or edges (update 54), and each section draws in a quiet colour of its own (the source ochre, the shaper teal, modulation violet, FX steel blue, AMP terracotta); the four knobs in dusty colours; thin lines with a faint glow. The type is IBM Plex Sans Condensed (SIL Open Font License) at its lighter weights. The header is the page's icon and name, with no tag.

  • The picture: a moving picture of what the page does to the sound: the source's wave, the filter's curve, the envelope, the LFO shapes, Rings' ripples, the delay's repeats. It fills most of every page that has one (update 50: the sliders below gave it more room). GLOBAL › ANIMATION sets it to full, calm or still.
  • The sliders (update 50, replacing the round dials): one per knob along the bottom, each with its knob's number and name, the value in large type, and a slider underneath.
    • Each value is a dot on a hairline in its knob's colour; an amount (cutoff, level, time) draws the line up to the dot a little brighter, a bipolar one (+/-) from the middle.
    • A choice is not an amount: a choice of two (ON/OFF, INT/EXT) is a switch; a short list or a small whole-number range shows a tick for each stop and a block at the one chosen; a list also shows which of how many (6/24) beside its name.
    • The sliders move with the sound, gently. While the FM-1 plays, each dot warms a little towards ivory as the sound gets louder, a faint halo breathes round it and brightens for a moment on each new hit, and now and then it bobs a pixel where the sound swings hardest. Each knob reads the sound at its own zoom, so the four move differently. When the sound stops they are still.
  • The help line: what the controls do on this page.
  • Messages appear for a moment in a grey band across the bottom, over the help line ("SAVED TO U01", "PLAITS 6 V", "CHORDS OFF").

Knob colours

Each knob keeps its colour on every page, its number drawn in it beside the name: K1 grey-blue, K2 sage, K3 sand, K4 clay. The part of the picture a knob moves is drawn in that colour, so turning the sage knob moves the sage part. The sequencer's four sliders take the same colours. For example:

  • AMP: attack, decay, sustain and release are four coloured segments of the envelope.
  • The filter: a dashed line at the cutoff and a dot on the resonance peak.
  • Plaits and Braids: the wave in TIMBRE's colour.
  • Rings: the guide rings in STRUCTURE's colour, the ripples in BRIGHTNESS's.
  • Delay, reverb, chorus: the repeats in FEEDBACK's colour, the reverb's cloud in SIZE's, the chorus' two lines in RATE's and DEPTH's.

On HOME the macros take the same colours: M1 grey-blue, M2 sage, M3 sand, M4 clay.

Modulation on the sliders

A parameter that something is moving shows it, in the colour of what moves it:

  • a glow round the slider shows the range it has swept lately, brighter the faster it moves;
  • the dot is where it is now, and the small number beside the value (› 62) is its live value;
  • a small tick on the hairline marks where you set it.

On the slider pages the same shows as a halo round the track.

5. HOME

HOME shows the preset's category, name and number (or its user slot), the sound's path, the four macros and the output.

  • The path: the slots in use, as live tiles in the order the sound goes through them: SRC › SHP › FX 1, then down to FX 2. Dots run along the arrows, as bright as the output. Empty slots are left out and the path closes up.
  • The tiles: each slot's module, moving with what it is doing to the sound right now. The colours tell the kinds apart: orange sources, teal shapers, purple modulators, blue effects.
    • A switched-off effect shows OFF (section 10).
    • A red tile is a module the preset needs that didn't fit in memory.
  • The modulators loaded in MOD A and MOD B sit under the path; small arrows pulse up from them as their outputs move.
  • The macros: each with its value (0–100), a slider in its knob's colour (moving with the sound, as on the pages), and what it moves (the first destination, "+2" if more), or UNSET. K1–K4 turn them; so do MIDI CC 20–23.
  • The tabs along the bottom show what's on besides the preset: the scale and chord, the arpeggiator, the STYLE, a tuning. A red tab on the left, KEYS: FM-1 OFF (or ARP: FM-1 OFF), means the keys or the arpeggio aren't playing the FM-1's sound (GLOBAL › CHORD + ARP › CHD FM-1, or two quick taps on ARP; section 10). (Update 39 took away the waveform that ran along the bottom, and HOME now only redraws the macros and tabs when they change, so it takes less of the processor's time.)

PRESET chooses the preset, ALGORITHM jumps a category and SELECT sets the voices (1–6). Changing preset, or loading a module into a slot, fades the sound out over a few milliseconds and the new one in, so it doesn't click; nothing of the old sound (its echoes, its last few samples) carries into the new one. Knobs glide to their new values over a few milliseconds too, so a level, a cutoff or a chorus turned quickly doesn't click or zipper; a choice from a list (a model, a type) still changes at once. Tap HOME again for RACK, a list of every slot, AMP, the outputs and how many matrix routes are set.

The chain

The filter and its SWEEP (update 26)

The filter is in every voice's chain, always (after the shaper, before AMP), not a module you load. ENV, then ENV again, opens its page (AMP's pages and its MOD page come first); the shaper (Rings or empty) is the page after it, and its picker no longer offers the filter. Its page has four pages of four: TYPE, CUTOFF, RESO, DRIVE; ENV AMT, KEY TRK, VEL AMT, ENV SRC; F ATK, F DEC, F SUS, F REL; SAT, AUDIO FM, GAIN and SWEEP. Every one of them can be a destination of the matrix and of the knob layer, listed as FLT. in the destinations (FLT.CUTOFF, FLT.SWEEP, ...).

A preset with no filter has it open. A low-pass with no resonance, drive, saturation, audio FM or gain whose cutoff is above the audio band (the CUTOFF at the top of its travel) is transparent: the filter fades out over the last 14 semitones below 21.6 kHz and is then skipped, so a sound that had no filter sounds exactly as it did (every one of the 46 factory presets plays sample for sample as before, with the SWEEP in the centre). The filter every other preset has is the same as it always was.

SWEEP is for your hand. It adds to the cutoff, the envelope, the matrix and the knob layer, never replacing them: the centre (0) is the preset's own cutoff; turn it down for darker, up for brighter, in the pitch-like (exponential) scale of the cutoff: the whole travel, -1 to +1, is 5 octaves each way (the display shows the octaves). The engine smooths it a block at a time, so a quick sweep does not zipper or click. Three ways to move it:

  • Hold ENV and turn K1, on any page (not the sequencer's, which have their own K1): the toast shows FILTER SWEEP and its value in octaves, CENTRE at 0. This takes no macro from the presets: K1-K4 stay the macros, and the sweep is only under K1 while ENV is down.
  • MIDI CC 74 (brightness, the standard one): 64 is the centre, 0 the bottom, 127 the top. It glides to each value. (K1 afterwards goes on from where the CC left it.)
  • The FILTER page's last parameter, SWEEP, and as a destination of the matrix or the knob layer.

SWEEP is saved by SAVE in a user slot with the rest of the filter and comes back with it; loading a factory preset puts it back in the centre. A user slot saved before update 26 plays as it was saved: its filter (the shaper's module 12) moves into the filter's own page, with the SWEEP in the centre. With Rings in the shaper slot, Rings is shared by the voices and the filter only takes the dry sound.

Module pages

Open a slot with its button. SELECT turns the pages within the module; K1–K4 set the four parameters shown.

Changing a module: on its page, turn ALGORITHM. The picker opens on the module now loaded. Turn to choose, then press the slot's button again to load it, or HOME to leave it as it was. Loading stops the sound for a moment while memory is laid out again. It then says how many voices fit ("BRAIDS 6 V"), or "NO ROOM".

Memory: some edits need more memory (a longer delay, Rings' STR+VERB, the shimmer reverb). When you stop turning, FM1 Rack lays the memory out again, with a moment's silence, and says MEMORY FITTED with the voices.

The MOD page: after a module's last page (and AMP's and OSC's) comes its MOD page. It lists the matrix routes into that module, four at a time, under the module's picture. SELECT steps down the rows. On the selected row, K1 sets the source, K2 the target (only this module's parameters, so the list is short), K3 the amount and K4 the via (section 8). On a free row, turning a knob adds a route.

UNDO: hold EDIT to put the last change back: a parameter, a matrix route, or a SEL, ARP or GLOBAL setting ("UNDO SRC.COLOR"). Hold it again to go further back, up to 16 changes. One turn of a knob counts as one change. Changing preset or loading a module starts afresh.

Edits are lost when you change preset unless you SAVE.

Clouds: the grain controls (update 27)

Clouds sits in FX 1 or FX 2 and listens to everything before it, so it granulates the FM-1's own sound, live. Its buffer is short, set by QUALITY (page 4): 0.77 s at 16 ST, 1.5 s at 16 MONO, 3.1 s at 8 ST, 6.1 s at 8 MONO (the 8-bit qualities are lo-fi). The grain controls shape GRANULR mode; LOOPDLY and SPECTRL ignore WINDOW, SPEED and JITTER.

The first two pages are the grain set. Every control is a matrix destination and a knob-layer target, and the three new ones are saved in user presets.

Page Knob Does
GRAIN 1 POSITION Where in the buffer grains start: 0 is the newest sound (right on the picture), 1 the oldest
WINDOW The width of the region around POSITION that grains start in: 0 is one point (Clouds as it was), 1 the whole buffer
SPEED A playhead that travels through the window by itself: centre stops it, right is forward (toward the newest sound), left reverse, up to 2x real time either way, finer near the centre. It wraps at the window's ends. With FREEZE on this scrubs the frozen buffer; with it off, the buffer is itself moving, and the playhead travels through the recent sound
JITTER Each grain starts at a random place over this share of the window (0 on the playhead, 1 anywhere in the window); chosen for every grain
GRAIN 2 SIZE, DENSITY Grain length; how many grains (none at the middle, as with Clouds)
RND PAN Each grain's random place in the stereo field (0 all centred); drawn for every grain. This is the old SPREAD under a clearer name
FREEZE ON holds the buffer

WINDOW has to be above 0 for SPEED and JITTER to do anything: the playhead needs a window to travel in. The older SPRAY (a random offset in the whole buffer, per control block) and SCAN (a playhead round the whole buffer) are still there, on the last pages, and add to these. The picture shows the window as two dashed lines with the playhead moving between them. A preset saved before update 27 loads with WINDOW, SPEED and JITTER at 0.

Rings on the FM-1

There is one Rings shared by all the voices, as on the hardware module. Every voice's sound excites it, and it rings up to two notes at once: each new note takes the next resonator, so a third note cuts off the oldest ring. The dry part of each voice (set by Rings' MIX) plays as well. Rings' parameters follow the last note played.

  • Models: STRING, FM VOICE and STR+VERB. MODAL, SYMPATH and SYM QNT are too heavy for the FM-1, so a preset asking for one plays STRING. For struck and bell sounds, Plaits' own MODAL and STRING models are the light alternatives.
  • STR+VERB adds Rings' own reverb to the strings. DAMPING then sets the strings' decay and the reverb's size together. It is much lighter on the processor than a separate reverb module.
  • Sample rate: to fit the processor, Rings runs at half the sample rate (24 kHz). Its tuning, decays and tone are kept, but it has nothing above 12 kHz.

7. AMP and OSC

AMP

ENV opens AMP, which has six pages:

  1. ATTACK, DECAY, SUSTAIN, RELEASE
  2. VEL AMT, GLIDE, SPREAD (stereo), LEVEL
  3. M ATTACK, M DECAY, M LOOP: the mod envelope (the matrix source MOD ENV)
  4. MODE (POLY, MONO, LEGATO, UNI 2, UNI 4), DETUNE, VINTAGE
  5. DELAY, HOLD, A CURVE, D CURVE
  6. VEL>ATK, KEY>TIME, M SUST, PITCH ENV

UNI 2 and UNI 4 play two or four detuned voices per key. The envelope is drawn on every page.

OSC: the second oscillator

Tap EDIT twice for OSC: a plain oscillator in every voice, mixed with the source before the shaper, so the filter or Rings, AMP and the effects take both.

  • Page 1: WAVE (SINE, TRI, SAW, SQUARE, PULSE, NOISE), OCTAVE (−3 to +2), SEMI (±12), FINE (±1 semitone).
  • Page 2, the mixer: PW (pulse width), OSC LVL (0, off, by default), MAIN LVL (the source's level, 100 by default), AUX LVL (Plaits' second output, on its own level; Plaits only).

With OSC LVL at 0 a preset sounds as it would without OSC. Several factory basses use OSC as a sub-oscillator an octave down.

8. Modulation

Anything in a preset can move anything else. The movers (sources) are:

  • the macros M1–M4;
  • VELO (how hard a key was played), KEY TRK (which key), RANDOM (a new value each note);
  • the envelopes AMP ENV and MOD ENV;
  • the modulators' outputs A OUT 1, A OUT 2, B OUT 1, B OUT 2;
  • the LFOs LFO 1–3;
  • the MSEQ lanes SEQ 1–4.

They reach their destinations three ways: the LFOs' own targets, the matrix (and each module's MOD page, which shows part of it), and LEARN.

LFO steps through MOD A, MOD B, LFO, MSEQ and the MATRIX. Tap it twice quickly to go straight to the MATRIX.

LFOs

The LFO page has eleven pages (SELECT):

  • pages 1–3: SHAPE (SINE, TRI, SAW, SQUARE, S&H, DRIFT), RATE, SYNC (FREE, or 1/16 to 2 BARS of the tempo), RETRIG, for LFO 1, 2 and 3;
  • pages 4–6: FADE (a fade-in from each note), PHASE, POLAR, 1SHOT, for each;
  • pages 7–9: LFO TARGET, each LFO's two direct destinations. K1 and K3 choose the destination, K2 and K4 the amount;
  • pages 10–11: the GATE PATTERN (below).

GATE PATTERN: a rhythm that fires triggers

LFO pages 10 and 11 are the ARP RHYTHM generator again, but its hits are gates, not notes. Two lanes, GATE 1 and GATE 2, each an Euclidean ring with its own RATE from the tempo (or MIDI clock). Nothing runs while a lane's RATE is OFF (the start), so patches sound as before.

  • Page 10, GATE PATTERN: LANE (GATE 1 or 2), RATE (OFF, 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32), HITS, STEPS. Help line: HITS FIRE A GATE: RETRIG LFO, ENV, MATRIX.
  • Page 11, GATE: CHANCE: LANE, TURN, CHANCE (odds of each hit), MUTATE.

Each hit raises the lane's gate for half a step. What listens:

  • The matrix: GATE 1 and GATE 2 are sources (1 while the gate is up, else 0).
  • LFO RETRIG: OFF, KEY, GATE 1, GATE 2; the LFO restarts on each hit.
  • PEAKS and TIDES: their GATE choice includes GATE 1 and GATE 2.
  • FUNC: TRIG includes GATE 1 and GATE 2.

The voices' own AMP and filter envelopes still start on notes; the gate retriggers the LFOs and the MOD slots' envelopes. The macros (knob layer) have no source list, so a gate reaches them only through a matrix row.

RATE runs from about 0.03 Hz to 30 Hz; at 75 it is about 5 Hz, a singer's vibrato.

The matrix

The MATRIX holds 12 routes. SELECT chooses the row. K1 sets the source, K2 the destination, K3 the amount (−100 to +100). K4 sets a via: a second source that scales the route. With VELO as the via, for example, harder notes modulate more.

Destinations are any parameter that can be modulated on SRC, SHP, MOD A, MOD B, FX 1, FX 2, AMP, OSC and FLT (the filter), plus pitch and pan.

By target: turn ALGORITHM to switch between ROUTES (the 12 rows) and TARGETS, the same routes sorted by what they move, so "what moves the cutoff?" reads off one place. SELECT moves along the list, and K1–K4 edit a route as on ROUTES.

Macros

M1–M4 are matrix sources you play with K1–K4 on HOME. Every factory preset sets them up so that at 50 the preset sounds as designed, and turning a macro moves its parameter either side.

LEARN: linking by turning a knob

  1. On HOME, turn the macro you want to link.
  2. Hold SEL and EDIT together for half a second. LEARN is on: the macro is framed in gold on HOME, HOME blinks, and a gold band replaces the help line on every page.
  3. Go to any module, AMP, LFO or OSC page and turn the knob of the parameter you want it to move. The link is made in the matrix, and how far you turned sets the amount: M2>SRC.TIMBRE +24.
  4. Turn more parameters to link more, then hold SEL and EDIT again to finish (tapping HOME also ends it).

Any source: while LEARN is on, turn ALGORITHM to choose what you're linking: a macro, velocity, key tracking, random, an envelope, a modulator's output, an LFO or an MSEQ lane. The gold band shows it. Then turn a parameter's knob as before. For example: hold SEL and EDIT, turn ALGORITHM to LFO 1, open the filter and turn CUTOFF: LFO 1>SHP.CUTOFF +20.

MSEQ: the modulation sequencer

MSEQ (on LFO, after the LFOs) has four lanes of 16 values that step with the tempo: the sources SEQ 1–4. SELECT chooses the step, ALGORITHM the lane, K1 the value (−100 to +100), K2 a shape to draw across the lane (RAMP+, RAMP−, TRI, SINE, SQR, RND, FLAT, INVERT, SHIFT), K3 the RATE (1/32 to 2 BARS), K4 SMOOTH.

9. FUNC: functions and a mixer

FUNC is a modulator for MOD A or MOD B, after Make Noise's Maths. To load it, open MOD A or B with LFO, turn ALGORITHM to FUNC, and press LFO. It has two function generators (channels 1 and 4), two inputs (channels 2 and 3) and a mixer, and gives two outputs to the matrix (A OUT 1 and A OUT 2 in MOD A).

10. Playing: SEL, ARP, shortcuts, GLOBAL and MIDI

SEL, ARP and GLOBAL are settings of the instrument, not of a preset. They stay when you change preset and are saved by themselves about 3 seconds after your last change.

SEL: scales, chords and the keys

Page 1, SCALE + CHORD:

  • SCALE (9 scales) and ROOT.
  • CHORD: OFF, TRIAD, 7TH, 9TH, 11TH, SUS4, 6TH, POWER. With a chord on, every key plays the scale's chord on that note, and the picture shows the chord and its name.
  • VOICING: CLOSE, LEAD, OPEN, INV1, INV2, +OCT.

Page 2, KEYS + STRUM:

  • QNT: snap notes to the scale; WHITE puts the scale on the white keys.
  • HARMONY.
  • ZONE: what the seven lowest keys (F3–B3) do:
    • CHORDS (to start with): with chords on, they colour the chord: add 6, m7, M7 or 9, or force DIM, MIN or MAJ. MIN and MAJ together give sus.
    • OFF: they play notes like the others.
    • BASS (with a STYLE on): they pick the chord tone the STYLE's bass line plays, from left to right root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, octave up, 5th below, approach note (a semitone under the root). The style still gives the rhythm and accents; the key held last wins; with none held the style plays its own bass line. With HELD, the held bass moves to the new tone at once. Switching ZONE back to CHORDS returns the bass to the style's own line.

  • STRUM: up to 80 ms between the chord's notes, lowest first. It also strums the chords the sequencer plays back (it's applied as they play, so turning it changes recorded chords too).

Page 4, FEEL: HUMAN (0–100, 0 to start with): each note of a chord comes a little late at random (up to 35 ms at 100) and a little softer or louder, so chords sound played rather than machine-tight. It works on the keys and on the chords the sequencer plays, together with STRUM, and is different every time.

The keys in the scale glow on the keybed.

ARP: the arpeggiator

  • Page 1: MODE (OFF, UP, DOWN, UPDN, DNUP, RANDOM, ORDER, CHORD, REPEAT, UP+8, CONV, DIVG, PINKY, THUMB, TURING), RATE (1/4 to 1/32, with triplets), OCTAVE (1–4), GATE.
  • Page 2: HOLD (latch), MUTATE and LOOP (for TURING, a looping random pattern), SWING.

It follows the tempo (GLOBAL › TEMPO) or MIDI clock, and the picture shows the pattern and the step playing.

Pages 3 and 4, RHYTHM and CHANCE (update 48; moved here from SEL, with plainer names): a ring generator for each layer. K1 (LAYER) picks the one to see and set: BASS (the master), CHORD or ARP; K2–K4 set its values. All three are drawn over each other round one centre (BASS outside, CHORD, ARP inside), the one picked bright and on top, the others dimmed behind; a legend beside them gives each one's pattern and hits. Steps are dots round the ring, the hits are joined into a shape, and a hand sweeps round as it plays. Each page has a one-line help on screen. The saved values are the same as before (the old ROTATE is now TURN, MODE is PATTERN, GEN is LAYER).

  • Page 3, RHYTHM: WHICH STEPS: HITS (how many), STEPS (the ring's length, 2–32), TURN (turns the pattern round). Hits are spread as evenly as they go (a Euclidean rhythm).
  • Page 4, CHANCE: HOW OFTEN: PATTERN, CHANCE (each hit's chance to play, 100% to start with), MUTATE (at the end of each cycle, the chance the ring turns a step or gains or loses a hit; a quarter of the time it goes back to the pattern as set).
  • PATTERN, BASS: STYLE (the style's own pattern, as before; TURN still turns it) or RING (HITS spread as evenly as possible round STEPS). CHORD and ARP: OFF (as before), RING (their own), SAME (the bass's hits) or ANSWER (the gaps between the bass's hits: call and response).

  • With CHORD on, the chord you hold is struck in the ring's rhythm instead of held. With ARP on, the arp rests on the ring's empty steps. BASS and CHORD need a STYLE on; ARP works whenever the arp runs.

HOLD as a chord latch: with HOLD on and MODE OFF, notes and chords keep sounding after you let go. Playing again once every key is up replaces them; keys added while others are held add to them. Turning HOLD off, changing MODE, or stopping the sequencer with PLAY ends them.

Chords and arpeggio: on the FM-1, and sent

What you play on the keys (a chord, or a single note with chords off) and what the arpeggiator plays each have two stages, switched separately:

  • ON FM-1: you hear it on the FM-1's own sound;
  • SEND: it goes out over MIDI on its own channel, GLOBAL › LAYER MIDI CH › CHORD CH (1 to start with) or ARP CH (3), so another synth can play it.

All four are on to start with. Switch them on GLOBAL's last page, CHORD + ARP, or with quick taps:

On the FM-1 Sent
Chords (SEL) on the page only three taps on SEL
Arpeggio (ARP) two taps on ARP three taps on ARP
On the page CHD FM-1 CHD SEND
ARP FM-1 ARP SEND

Each tap gesture shows what it did, for example CHORDS: SEND OFF or ARP: ON FM-1 OFF, and while one is off HOME shows a red KEYS: FM-1 OFF or ARP: FM-1 OFF tab, so a silent FM-1 is never a mystery.

Notes already sounding stop at once when you switch their stage off. With ON FM-1 off and SEND on, the FM-1 is silent and the other synth plays; with both off, nothing plays. SEND needs GLOBAL › MIDI OUT on (the message says so if it's off). The arpeggio's switches only matter while the arpeggiator is running (ARP › MODE).

These work with or without a STYLE. Two quick taps on SEL don't silence the keys: they turn the chords off, so the keys play single notes (two taps again bring the chord type back). To turn the arpeggiator off, use ARP › MODE.

STYLE: chords, bass and arpeggio on three channels

SEL's pages 3–5 (the MIDI controller pages; they were on ARP before update 38) turn the FM-1 into an accompaniment for itself and for other synths. While you hold a chord, it goes out in three layers:

  • CHORD: the chord, as you play it: it starts when you press the keys (strummed, if SEL › STRUM is set) and holds for as long as you hold them;
  • BASS: a bass line built from the chord's root (root, fifth, third, seventh, octave, passing and approach notes), in the style's rhythm. The style shapes the bass only;
  • ARP: the arpeggiator as it runs, when its MODE (page 1) is on.

Each layer goes out on its own MIDI channel, and you choose which ones the FM-1 plays itself (the chords and the arpeggio as in the section above). With a layer's FM-1 switch off, you don't hear it on the FM-1: it goes only to the other synth. So the FM-1 can play the chords and the arpeggio while another synth takes the bass, or you can send everything out and let the FM-1 play nothing but what you choose.

Page 3, STYLE:

  • STYLE: OFF, HELD (the bass holds the chord's root, with no rhythm) or one of 32 rhythms for the bass (below).
  • BASS OCT: the bass's octave, −2 to +1.
  • ACCENT: how much louder the accented bass notes (the roots marked as accents) are than the others.
  • BASS LEN: the bass notes' length, from short and punchy to legato.

GLOBAL page 9, LAYER MIDI CH (moved from SEL): CHORD CH, BASS CH and ARP CH, each OFF or 1–16 (1, 2 and 3 to start with), and BASS FM1: ON (you hear the bass on the FM-1, and it goes out too) or OFF (it goes out only, to start with). The bass is sent whenever its channel is set.

GLOBAL page 10, CHORD + ARP (moved from SEL): the chords' and the arpeggio's FM-1 and SEND switches (above). (If you saved your settings with update 34 or 35, ARP FM-1 may still be OFF: two taps on ARP turn it on.)

How it plays:

  • The bass rhythm starts from its first step when you press a chord with nothing held, at the tempo (GLOBAL › TEMPO) or on MIDI clock, and stops when you let go. With ARP › HOLD on, the bass keeps playing the last chord's root until you press another, stop with PLAY, or change a STYLE setting.
  • With SEL's CHORD on, the bass follows the latest key you pressed. With CHORD OFF, the keys you hold together make the chord, and the lowest is the bass's root.
  • GLOBAL › MIDI OUT must be on for the layers to reach other synths. With STYLE OFF, there's no bass; the chords and the arpeggio still go out on their channels when their SEND is on.
  • The picture on the STYLE page shows the style's bass rhythm (taller bars are the accented roots), with the step playing lit; the layer pages show where each layer goes. HOME shows the style's name in a tab at the bottom.

Shortcuts while you play

These gestures change things without opening a menu:

  • Two quick taps on SEL: chords off (single notes) or back on; three taps: the chords' send off or on.
  • Two quick taps on ARP: the arpeggio on the FM-1 off or on; three taps: its send off or on. (See Chords and arpeggio: on the FM-1, and sent, above.)
  • Two quick taps on FX: JAM on or off, from any page (see JAM: moves on the keys, below). The first tap opens FX 1 as always, the second, within 0.3 s, takes you back to the page you were on.
  • Hold FX, let go, then tap FX: after the hold the message says TAP FX 1, TWO TAPS FX 2. Within 2 seconds:
    • one tap turns FX 1 off or on;
    • two quick taps turn FX 2 off or on;
    • no tap leaves both as they were (NOTHING CHANGED).

A switched-off effect shows OFF on its HOME tile and costs no processor time. Its settings stay, and when you turn it back on it starts clean, with no old echoes. Loading a preset turns both effects on again; this on/off isn't saved with a preset. UNDO puts back the chord and arpeggio switches, as it does a knob turn.

JAM: moves on the keys (update 28)

JAM turns the upper keys into moves: each key throws the sound into a grain echo, a stutter, a glitch, a wash, a filter dive and so on, for as long as you hold it (or until you press it again). The keys below the split keep playing notes, so you play a chord or a line with your left hand and throw the sound about with your right.

In and out. Tap FX twice, quickly (within 0.3 s), from any page: the message says JAM ON and a gold JAM tag sits in the top strip, where the voice mode was (it is brighter while a move is sounding). Two quick taps again: JAM OFF, SOUND RESTORED. You stay on the page you were on (the first tap of the two opens FX 1 for a moment, as a single tap always does, and the second takes you back). JAM is also the third page of the FX button, after FX 1 and FX 2: tap FX until it comes up, or SELECT through its seven pages. The other FX gestures are as they were: one tap FX 1, tap again for FX 2, hold-let go-tap for the effects' on and off.

The split. The keyboard has 27 keys. By default the lower 15 play notes and the upper 12 (G#4 to G5) are the twelve moves, in the order of the table below, left to right. Change the split on the JAM page (SPLIT KEY, 1 to 26: the first key that is a move; keys count from 0 at the lowest F). A split higher than 15 leaves the last moves without a key; a lower one, with more than twelve keys above it, goes round again. The octave buttons still move the notes, not the moves. Notes that come in over MIDI are not touched. With SEL's chord ZONE on, its seven lowest keys stay the zone keys. A move key is never a note, and does not start the arpeggiator, a chord or STYLE.

The moves. Each key shows the move's name when it is pressed (and HOLD, LATCH ON or LATCH OFF). A move changes only the sound's existing parameters, as an offset on top of the preset's own values (like the knob layer), and ignores a module the preset does not have:

Key Move What it does
1 GRAIN DELAY The sound thrown back in smeared, pitched echoes: with Clouds, BLEND, FEEDBACK, DENSITY and an octave of PITCH up; with a delay, its FEEDBACK, MIX and WOW up; the chorus and the reverb swell, the LFOs speed up
2 STUTTER 1/8 The voices and the chorus, delay and reverb wet chopped on the beat: open half of each eighth, silent the other half (the gate's edges are about 10 ms, so no clicks). With Clouds, a frozen slice of short grains plays through the gaps
3 STUTTER 1/16 The same at sixteenths
4 STUTTER 1/32 The same at thirty-seconds
5 REVERSE SCRUB With Clouds: FREEZE on, SPEED reverse (about 1.1x real time), a wide WINDOW, BLEND up: the buffer read backwards. Without Clouds there is no reverse; the reverb swells and the filter opens under it over two beats
6 TAPE STOP The pitch falls toward two octaves down, over a second or so, the filter closes a little, the LFOs and Clouds' speed slow, the delay's WOW swings; let go and it springs back in 0.15 s
7 WASH The reverb's MIX, SIZE, SHIMMER and DAMP swell (about 0.6 s), the filter softens, the delay and chorus join; with Clouds, REVERB and FEEDBACK up
8 FREEZE PAD With Clouds: FREEZE on, WINDOW and JITTER wide, BLEND up: the sound held and smeared. Without it: the reverb and delay feedback pushed up for a long smear (the held sound itself cannot be frozen without Clouds)
9 GLITCH Every sixteenth a new random draw: a pitch jump (an octave, a fifth or a fourth, up or down), a drop-out, a crushed bit, a filter jump; with Clouds, a new POSITION and SIZE too
10 FILTER DIVE The cutoff falls about four and a half octaves over about a second, the RESONANCE and DRIVE rise
11 FILTER RISE From nearly closed, the cutoff opens over twelve sixteenths to a little brighter than the sound's own, with a resonant peak on the way; let go and it returns in 0.15 s
12 CRUSH Braids' BITS and RATE drop (down to 4 bits and 16 kHz at full amount and a hard press) and the filter's DRIVE rises

The stutters, GLITCH and FILTER RISE are clocked: they count sixteenths from the moment you press, at the tempo (GLOBAL), or at the MIDI clock's when GLOBAL's CLOCK is set to MIDI. What a move can do depends on the preset: the modules a move names are the ones the preset has. Of the 46 factory presets, 45 have a reverb, 27 a chorus, 16 a delay and one (TIDE PAD) Clouds, so on most presets GRAIN DELAY and FREEZE PAD are chorus and reverb swells, and REVERSE SCRUB and the grain parts of the stutters need Clouds (load it into an FX slot with ALGORITHM on the FX page to hear them). The voices' level, the filter (and its SWEEP), the pitch, Braids' BITS and RATE are in every preset.

HOLD or LATCH. Each move has its own switch (the KEY knob on its page): HOLD (the default) is on while the key is down; LATCH turns it on with one press and off with the next, so you can leave a wash running and play on. Each move also has an AMOUNT (0 to 100 %, default 80, WASH and FREEZE PAD 70), and your velocity scales it (a gentle press is about 40 % of a hard one). Moves stack: hold several at once and their offsets add.

Letting go. A move glides in and out at a speed of its own (the stutters in 8 ms, the filter dive over about half a second, TAPE STOP's fall over a second or so, a wash out over 0.6 s). When it has gone, every parameter is exactly the sound's own again, not nearly. Turning JAM off, loading another preset, changing the SPLIT KEY or a move's HOLD or LATCH switch ends every move at once (a latched move included); loading a preset leaves JAM on. Nothing JAM does is written into the preset: a knob you turn meanwhile, SAVE, the matrix and the knob layer see the preset's own values.

The JAM page. Page 1: JAM (ON or OFF) and SPLIT KEY. Pages 2 to 7: two moves to a page, each its AMOUNT and its KEY (HOLD or LATCH), named in the header (GRAIN + STUT 8, STUT 16 + 32, SCRUB + TAPE, WASH + FREEZE, GLITCH + DIVE, RISE + CRUSH). The picture shows the twelve moves, each as a bar that rises while it is on, a tick at its amount, and a gold dot under a latched one. UNDO takes back a JAM setting like any other. JAM's settings are not kept across a power-up (JAM starts off, SPLIT KEY 15, the default amounts). It was tested on a computer and not yet heard on an FM-1.

USB audio (update 57)

Connected to a computer by USB, the FM-1 shows up as an audio device ("FM1 RACK") beside its MIDI port:

  • Recording: choose the FM-1 as the input in your DAW or audio app; it sends exactly what comes out of its headphone and line output, after MASTER.
  • Playback: choose the FM-1 as the output, and the computer's sound plays through the FM-1's output, mixed with its own. The recording never includes what the computer plays back, so you can monitor a backing track through the FM-1 while you record it.

On a Mac it may take unplugging and plugging back in the first time, for the computer to see the new audio device. The FM-1 records at 44.1 kHz: set your DAW (or Audio MIDI Setup on a Mac) to 44.1 kHz for it, or the computer converts the rate on the way in. The recording keeps about 12 ms in hand (update 58), so it stays clean while the FM-1 works hard on a heavy preset. If the sound crackles while you record a heavy preset, the processor is the limit, not the USB: try a lighter preset or fewer voices.

MIDI

In, over USB or TRS, on GLOBAL › MIDI CH:

  • notes, through SEL and the arpeggiator like the keys;
  • pitch bend and sustain (CC 64);
  • program change, when PROG CHG is on (factory presets, then yours);
  • CC 120 and 123 (all notes off);
  • CC 20–23 for the macros, and CC 74 for the filter's SWEEP (64 the centre);
  • clock, start and stop, when CLOCK is MIDI: the arpeggiator, the sequencer and the tempo follow it. (Update 59) The FM-1 follows the clock with a steady internal clock that the incoming ticks keep in step, so a little jitter in the clock, or in when the FM-1 reads it, doesn't make the groove wobble: notes land within about half a millisecond of the beat, a constant 3 ms behind it. It locks within a beat of the clock starting or changing tempo.

Out, over USB, when GLOBAL › MIDI OUT is on (off by default):

  • the keys' notes and chords on CHORD CH and the arpeggio on ARP CH, when their SEND is on; the STYLE bass on BASS CH; the sequencer on OUT CH (MIDI that came in isn't echoed);
  • clock on the internal tempo;
  • with +CC, the macros as CC 21–24.

11. The sequencer

Tap SEQ to open it; it remembers the page you were on. Tap SEQ again, or turn SELECT, to step through its six pages: NOTES, PATTERN, SPICE, GEN, BANKS, SONG (and the seven drum pages after them, see DRUMS below). On each, the four values sit as sliders along the top and the rest of the screen is the piano roll or the page's picture.

  • PLAY starts and stops it from any page. Stopping also ends held notes (latched chords and the arpeggiator's HOLD).
  • It plays at GLOBAL › TEMPO or on MIDI clock, and sends its notes to MIDI out when that's on.
  • There are 8 banks (A–H) of up to 64 steps. Each step holds up to 6 notes, each with its own length, dynamics (PP, MP, MF, FF, FFF) and articulation (none, staccato, tenuto, accent, marcato), plus a ratchet, nudge, slide, chance and fill condition.

NOTES: writing steps

  • Step entry: with the sequencer stopped, play a note or a chord and let go. It's written into the step under the cursor, and the cursor moves on.
  • SELECT turns the sequencer's pages; ALGORITHM moves the cursor four steps.
  • K1 walks through every step, a click each: a step with a note picks it (highlighted), a step with a chord picks each of its notes in turn before moving on, an empty step is passed with nothing picked. K2 the picked note's pitch, K3 its length, K4 its DYNAMICS: PP, MP, MF, FF, FFF.
  • Hold SEL and turn K4: the note's articulation instead (the slider reads ARTIC while SEL is held), drawn over the note:
    • none;
    • staccato .: half as long;
    • tenuto _: its full length, a little louder;
    • accent >: a dynamic louder;
    • marcato (an accent standing up, like a tepee ^): two dynamics louder and a little short.

  • Only EDIT deletes: the note K1 picked (NOTE DELETED). Turning K4 down stops at PP.
  • Lasso: hold SEL and turn K1: the steps from where you started to where you turn are picked, lit gold. Then:
    • turn K1 to move the whole group a step a click, K2 to move all its notes up or down a semitone a click, K3 to make all of them longer or shorter, K4 to change all their dynamics, and hold SEL and turn K4 for all their articulations (a message shows the new value);
    • EDIT clears every note in them;
    • hold SAVE copies them (COPIED 3 STEPS; up to 32).
  • Paste: move the cursor (ALGORITHM, or K1 to a note), then hold SAVE with nothing lassoed: what you copied is written from the cursor's step on (PASTED). A tap of SAVE still saves the sequencer.
  • Note names follow the key signature (SEL's ROOT and SCALE): sharps in G, D, A, E, B and F# major and their relative minors, flats in the others, C included (B♭, E♭...).
  • To finish with a lasso, tap SEL (LASSO OFF): the notes stay where you left them and K1–K4 work on single notes again. Turning ALGORITHM, or SELECT to another page, also ends it.

  • The roll has a lane for each semitone, a see-through keyboard down its left edge with the C's named, and notes as tall as their lane and at least three quarters of a step wide.

PATTERN

K1 LENGTH (1–64 steps), K2 DIV (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 8T, 16T, 1/2, 1 BAR, 2 BAR, 4 BAR), K3 SWING, K4 GATE. ALGORITHM moves the cursor over the step grid.

SPICE

K1 SPICE adds random rests, octave jumps and force changes, more as you turn it up. K2 DICE rolls the same amount differently. K3 CHANCE sets how often the step under the cursor plays. K4 FILL: –, FILL (only in a fill) or NO FILL (only outside one).

GEN: pattern tools

K1 chooses the tool: EUCLID, RANDOM, ACID, ARP UP, ARP UPDN, BERLIN, OCTAVES, OFFBEAT, ROLLING, STABS, PROGRESS, GATES, SHIFT >, SHIFT <, REVERSE, DOUBLE, HALF or CLEAR. K2 and K3 are its two values (for EUCLID, pulses and rotation, with a ring showing the result), and K4 is the seed. Turn ALGORITHM right to apply it, left to put the bank back. New notes follow SEL's scale and key.

BANKS

K1 picks a bank. While playing, the switch waits for the end of the loop (the queued bank blinks) unless K4 SWITCH is NOW. K2 chooses an action (COPY TO, CLEAR, DOUBLE, HALVE) and K3 the target bank for COPY TO. ALGORITHM right does it, left undoes it.

SONG

16 rows, each a bank with how many times it repeats (1–16) and a transposition (±12). K1 chooses the row, K2 the bank (END stops the song there), K3 REPEATS, K4 TRANSP. ALGORITHM right turns the song on or off; ALGORITHM left turns your jam (the banks you've just played, in order) into the song.

Recording

  • Stopped: tap REC for REC READY. Then PLAY counts you in (GLOBAL › CLICK › COUNT IN: a bar of four clicks to start with; the header reads COUNT IN) and recording starts on the next downbeat, with the click going on while you record. Or just play: the first note starts the sequencer recording straight away, with no count-in. PLAY during the count-in stops it; REC cancels REC READY.

  • Playing: REC turns recording on and off. What you play is written into the steps as they pass.

The step layer

Hold SEQ (and keep holding) to turn the 16 white keys into steps:

  • tap a key to switch that step on (with the notes you last played) or off;
  • hold a step's key and turn K1 NOTE, K2 DYNAMICS, K3 RATCHET, K4 NUDGE;
  • OCT− / OCT+ show the previous or next 16 steps.

Let go of SEQ to play again.

DRUMS

Six drum lanes (KICK, SNARE, C HAT, O HAT, CLAP, PERC) of 16 steps play beside the note sequencer, on four voices of the FM-1's own processor (the two hats share one voice, and the clap and perc share one). They start and stop with PLAY, at the same tempo or MIDI clock, and each lane's SEND sets how much of it goes through the FX chain. Seven more pages follow SONG when you tap SEQ: DRUM, LANE SOUND, LANE MIX, LANE FEEL, DRUM GEN, DRUM MIDI, DRUM KIT (they have their own row of dots).

  • DRUM is the grid. SELECT moves along the steps, K1 chooses the lane, EDIT switches a step on or off, and K2, K3 and K4 set the step's force (GHOST to ACCENT), ratchet (1 to 4) and chance. Holding SEQ turns the white keys into the lane's 16 steps; turn a knob while holding a key to set that step, and K4 there is the nudge (a step early or late by up to 3/64). ALGO right mutes the lane; ALGO left clears it. A fresh grid is empty and shows the hint SEL+ALGO: LOAD BEAT: hold SEL and turn ALGO left on an empty grid and a basic beat is written (kick on the beats, snare and clap on 2 and 4, hats, a little perc). SEL + K1 chooses the pattern.
  • LANE SOUND gives the lane a model from a list that shows what each costs in CPU points, and relabels TONE and COLOUR for that model; K4 is the PITCH. SEL + ALGO loads a whole kit: 909 ISH, 808 ISH, LO-FI, SPARSE. Turning anything plays the lane once, so you hear it.
  • LANE MIX: DECAY, LEVEL, PAN and SEND (how much goes to the FX).
  • LANE FEEL: each lane's LENGTH (1 to 16 steps), DIVIDE (1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 8T, 16T), SHIFT and CHOKE (the lane it cuts when it hits: a closed hat cutting the open one). A lane of 12 against one of 16 drifts and comes back every bar, which is the point.
  • DRUM GEN fills a lane with EUCLID, FOUR FLOOR, BACKBEAT, OFFBEAT, 16THS, RANDOM, BREAK or CLEAR: K2 HITS, K3 TURN (rotate), K4 VARY (how many hits get another force). Turn ALGO right to write it; the lane is then edited by hand as any other. There is no undo (it would cost RAM), so write it into a lane you can spare.
  • DRUM MIDI: per lane OUT (SOUND, MIDI, ALSO or OFF), channel and GM note (the defaults are channel 10, kick 36, snare 38, closed hat 42, open hat 46, clap 39, perc 45) and IN. With IN on, a note on that lane's channel and note plays the lane (velocity sets its level) and is not played by the synth; every other note is the synth's.
  • DRUM KIT: the pattern chips A to H (turn ALGO right to queue one; while the drums play it takes over at the next bar line and every lane restarts, and an edit before then calls it off; ALGO left calls the queue off), the KIT, SWING (50 to 75 %, on the 16ths; triplets are not swung) and IF BUSY, what the drums do when the processor is short of room: CLOUD (the default: Clouds' DENSITY is held at 0.9 while the drums play), VOICES (a synth voice is shed) or NONE. The page shows the load of the current kit in points. SELECT turns on whether STYLE starts and stops the drums with it (on by default).

Changing the pattern while playing is refused when the live pattern has unsaved edits, because it would be lost: press SAVE first.

The patterns A to H, the kit, the lane sounds, feel, MIDI settings, swing and IF BUSY are kept by SAVE on any drum page (they stay in memory until then). An older save with no drums loads as before, with an empty grid.

Keeping the sequencer

The banks, song and MSEQ lanes stay in memory while the FM-1 is on. To keep them after switching off, press SAVE on a sequencer page. The sound stops for a moment while the flash is written, which is why FM1 Rack never saves the sequencer by itself while it plays.

12. Saving and the library

There are 64 user presets. A user preset keeps the six modules and their parameters, AMP, OSC, the LFOs, all the routes, the macros, the tempo, a name of up to 10 characters and a category.

Saving

Press SAVE for the NAME + SLOT page:

Control Does
SELECT the letter to change
ALGORITHM or K1 change the letter (A–Z, 0–9, −, #, .)
K2 / K3 / K4 the slot / the category / the page of 16 slots
SAVE store it in that slot
OCT+ the library
HOME close

The preset you saved comes back at power-on.

The library

From SAVE, press OCT+. K1 (or PRESET) picks a row, K2 the tab (ALL, USR, BS, PD, LD, CH, FX, OTH), K3 the action (LOAD, COPY, RENAME, DELETE). OCT+ does it (DELETE asks twice). OCT− or SAVE goes back to NAME; HOME closes.

13. Voices and the processor

The FM-1 has two processor cores. FM1 Rack shares the voices between them: the first core also runs Rings and the reverb, so the second takes more of the voices.

  • Voices: each preset starts with its own number of voices, up to 6, within what fits in memory and time. SELECT on HOME (or GLOBAL › VOICES) asks for 1–6; FM1 Rack keeps to what fits.
  • SHED n: if a block of sound takes more than 90 % of its time, FM1 Rack drops a voice and the status strip shows SHED n.
  • TOO HEAVY FOR FM-1: a preset too heavy even at one voice is muted, with the message at the bottom of HOME and HEAVY in red on the status strip, so the screen and controls keep working. Pick another preset.
  • What costs most: Plaits voices cost more than Braids voices, and the reverb and Rings are the heaviest modules. The reverb and the delay also use the most memory, so presets with both may get fewer voices. Switching an effect off (section 10) frees its time.

14. Factory presets

There are 45 (plus INIT, number 0): 9 pads and drones, 9 keys, bells and plucks, 6 thumb pianos, 6 leads, 7 basses and 8 textures. PRESET steps through INIT, the 45 in the order below, and on to your own 15 slots; ALGORITHM jumps between categories.

Every preset:

  • has four knobs, K1 to K4, that are macros: each moves 4 to 8 parameters at once, gently at the bottom and towards glitch or a wash at the top, in its own direction (the names under the picture on HOME are the preset's own: the table below gives them as K1 · K2 · K3 · K4);
  • also moves on its own (LFOs, Tides, Func, Peaks, Grids or Rings, set per preset); the "movement" column says how much at rest: subtle (steady, a slow drift), medium (movement you notice) or bold (it plainly changes);
  • is in tune (A4 = 440) within a few cents from C2 to C5, and level-matched to the others, so you can switch without touching MASTER;
  • responds to velocity in level and tone.

Nothing in this bank has been heard on an FM-1: every figure behind it is a desktop render of the engine.

Older saves. A user preset saved on the older 125-preset bank keeps working: its base is mapped to the nearest preset still in the bank, or to INIT where there is none, and it plays as it was saved. The preset you last played is carried over the same way. The 16 granular presets and 73 older ones were removed from the list.

15. Troubleshooting

What you see What to do
No sound, TOO HEAVY FOR FM-1 The preset needs more than one core for one voice. Pick another preset, or swap its heaviest module
SHED n on the status strip Voices were dropped for time. Play fewer notes, lower the voices, switch an effect off, or choose lighter modules
No effect on a preset It may be switched off: hold FX, let go, then one or two taps; or check the HOME tile for OFF
Chords or the arpeggiator won't stop PLAY ends latched notes; ARP › MODE OFF stops the arpeggiator
The keys are silent (except while recording), or HOME shows KEYS: FM-1 OFF The chords' FM-1 stage is off: GLOBAL › CHORD + ARP › CHD FM-1 ON (update 38 turns it back on once). Also check GLOBAL › MIDI › LOCAL is ON
The arpeggio is silent, or HOME shows ARP: FM-1 OFF Two quick taps on ARP (or GLOBAL › CHORD + ARP › ARP FM-1)
Another synth gets nothing GLOBAL › MIDI OUT on; three quick taps on SEL or ARP turn SEND on; check CHORD CH / ARP CH
A moment of silence when loading a module or editing a delay or reverb Normal: memory is being laid out again (MEMORY FITTED)
A short gap when pressing SAVE on a sequencer page Normal: the flash is being written
A page opens a moment after the tap Normal on buttons with a hold or a double tap: the tap acts once it can't be either
The installer can't find the FM-1 Plug it straight into the Mac (no hub), quit DAWs and Web MIDI tabs, run the script again
The FM-1 won't start after an install Follow section 2, "Getting back to the stock firmware"
A preset sounds wrong Note its name and what you hear, and report it: not every preset has been heard on an FM-1 yet
